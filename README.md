# TITLE OF THE PRODUCT

Yuvan-Sara Tool Factory is equipped and organised to manufacture a wide range of precision machined components. The three VMC machines, combined with the structured inspection and handling workflow, provide the capability to produce components of varying complexity — from simple prismatic blocks and flanges to geometrically complex engine components. The facility specialises in the following representative product families:

(a)	Piston Head
The piston head is a cylindrical engine component machined from aluminium alloy or cast iron billet. The manufacturing sequence involves face milling of the crown surface (VMC MC 01), ring groove profiling (VMC MC 02), and precision bore machining of the gudgeon pin hole to h6 tolerance (VMC MC 03). Critical tolerance requirements include the piston outer diameter for correct bore clearance, ring groove width and depth, and gudgeon pin bore diameter. The multi-operation nature of piston head machining makes the parallel VMC configuration at Yuvan-Sara particularly suitable, as different operations can be distributed across machines to balance cycle times and reduce total throughput time.

(b) Connecting Rod
The connecting rod transmits the reciprocating motion of the piston to the rotational motion of the crankshaft. It is one of the most geometrically complex components produced at the facility, requiring milling of the I-beam shank profile (VMC MC 01), precision boring of the big-end and small-end bores to H7 tolerance (VMC MC 02), drilling and tapping of the cap bolt holes, and precision surface finishing (VMC MC 03). The dedicated fixture area supports the multi-face fixturing setups required for this component. The oil slot visible in the 3D model adds a contouring operation to the machining sequence, demonstrating the 2.5D capability of the VMC fleet.

(c)	Crankshaft
 The crankshaft is the most complex representative product, requiring the highest number of machining operations and the tightest geometric tolerances in the engine assembly. The manufacturing process involves rough profiling of the cheek and counterweight geometries (VMC MC 01), precision milling of the main journal and crankpin diameters (VMC MC 02), and finishing of the oil holes, keyway features, and the flange bolt circle (VMC MC 03). The crankshaft demands particularly rigorous inspection after each major machining stage, making the dedicated inspection room and inspection team essential to maintaining conformance throughout the multi-stage production sequence. Given its complexity, the crankshaft represents the design ceiling of the facility's current capability and serves as a benchmark for VMC performance qualification.

# DESCRIPTION

In modern manufacturing industries, efficient factory planning and process optimisation play a major role in improving productivity, reducing operational costs, and ensuring smooth workflow. DELMIA, developed by Dassault Systemes, is an advanced digital manufacturing and simulation software widely used for factory layout planning, workflow simulation, robotic operations, ergonomics, and production system analysis. It enables engineers to create a virtual model of the factory environment — including machines, workstations, workers, material storage areas, and movement paths — before the actual physical setup is implemented.
Factory flow planning is an important aspect of industrial engineering because it defines how raw materials move through different stages of production such as machining, inspection, assembly, and final storage. A well-designed factory flow reduces unnecessary movement, minimises material handling time, avoids congestion, improves worker efficiency, and ensures better utilisation of available floor space. Proper arrangement of CNC machines, inspection zones, and storage locations also supports continuous production with minimum delay.
The use of simulation before real-time implementation is highly beneficial because it allows engineers to identify design problems, workflow interruptions, machine placement issues, and safety concerns in a risk-free virtual environment. Instead of changing the layout after installation, the complete system can be tested, modified, and optimised digitally. This reduces the chances of costly errors and helps in selecting the most efficient production arrangement.
Another major advantage of DELMIA simulation is the reduction in cost and time. Since the production line is validated virtually, industries can reduce machine relocation costs, lower setup time, improve manpower allocation, and shorten project development cycles. It also supports better decision-making by providing a clear visualisation of worker movement, machine utilisation, and material flow, ultimately leading to higher productivity and better space optimisation.
